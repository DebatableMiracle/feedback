Honestly, being here in a new college didn't really feel all that. But one of the coolest things that happened in the 2 weeks was meeting this RL professor, and in one of his slides, he talks about how yann leCunn called RL "cherry on top of the Supervised Learning cake" which he also jokingly pointed LeCunn working on World models is technically working on work derived from RL only. And I could see he also doesn't agree with these demeaning statements about RL. 

While Ik RL doesn't work that well even now. But I still strongly feel after all the reading that RL is the next AI step in some ways. I don't like the idea of fine tuning with RL though, seems a little short term solution to me. But RL explores about Human behaviour of Learning which Supervised Learning can't imagine. I feel the opposite about RL than yann in some ways. As Sutton and barto write in their 2018 book:

>The artificial neural network provides the program with the ability to generalize from its experience, so that in new states it selects moves based on information saved from similar states faced in the past, as determined by its network. How well a reinforcement learning system can work in problems with such large state sets is intimately tied to how appropriately it can generalize from past experience. It is in this role that we have the greatest need for supervised learning methods with reinforcement learning. Artificial neural networks and deep learning are not the only, or necessarily the best, way to do this.

RL is what needs a Supervised Learning method that can move Physical AI into developing much richer understanding of our World.
