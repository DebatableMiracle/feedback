Pondering over interesting research directions to take in robotics, carla n stuff


- **Asynchronous multimodal interaction:** Robots shouldn't require synchronized sensor inputs. Vision, touch, proprioception, etc. can arrive at different times, disappear, or become more important depending on the interaction. The robot maintains a persistent understanding of the world and updates it from whatever sensory information arrives.
    
- **Online adaptation to the environment through uncertainty and interaction:** When the environment differs from expectations—fog, slippery roads, unexpected object properties—the robot detects uncertainty/prediction errors and actually changes behaviour: slow down, probe, become cautious, shift sensor reliance, etc.
    
- **Long-term memory of physical experiences:** The robot remembers important environment-specific interactions and adaptations, so if it encounters a similar situation or environment again, it doesn't have to rediscover everything from scratch. (part of the adaptation in last case.)
- 
I think large items of these are about how slowly robots and models learn currently and adapt as well. Learning in current AI is a very slow process. Large datasets are needed while we understand the world very quickly through correlations, like water's slippery oil is slippery, then we only focus on how to learn the coefficient of slipperiness and also the idea of how to move in that coefficient (part of our motor skills adaptation) Robotic data unfortunately isn't as good. Currently there are not systems that work on how predictable the environment is, if we see something new, we try to learn it and act more cautious but How do we make robots learn this "skill"?
